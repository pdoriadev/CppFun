What replaced what

  • The separate time slider is gone. The strip itself is the control: click or drag on the ruler or any track row and the playhead follows the pointer. That is the "progress the time within it" you asked for — one surface that both shows where you are and sets it.
  • Transport, left to right: rewind to 0 · jump to the previous key on the selected track · back one frame · play/pause · forward one frame · next key · jump to the end. The two key-jump buttons are the ones you will actually use while placing keys; frame stepping is for checking an interpolation.
  • Time and Length stay as typeable fields. Scrubbing is fast but not precise, and setting a key at exactly 2.5 s needs a number. Length is the existing field, unmoved.

What the strip shows

  • One row per animated shape, newest at the bottom. The selected shape's row is highlighted so you can tell which track the key buttons act on.
  • Diamonds are keys, at their time. Read-only in this version — you can look at them, not drag them. Dragging keys means hit-testing, drag thresholds, undo, and collision when two keys land on the same frame; worth doing, but as its own change once the strip is proven.
  • Rows are 20 px, so a scene with more than about eight animated shapes will want the strip to scroll. Below that it costs about 60 px of panel height total, replacing the ~24 px the slider took.

The thing to look at

At 360 px the whole panel is about 344 px of usable width. A 5-second timeline is therefore ~68 px per second, and a key at 1.00 s sits 4.5 px from a key at 0.93 s. That is fine for seeing structure and rough scrubbing, and too tight for precise work — which is why the numeric Time field stays.

Answered, and built that way:
  1. All tracks, scrolling once there are more than six. A shape earns its row by being keyed — it appears when its first keyframe is placed, not when it is selected.
  2. 1/30 s frame step.
